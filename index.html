Smart Email Assist

Bringing generative AI into the sales workflow — six weeks, zero precedent, and a market that wouldn't wait.

Salespeople spend roughly 28% of their time on non-selling work — chiefly reading and writing emails. In November 2022 I joined our product team shadowing Outreach sales reps in London, watching that pain up close. A month later, ChatGPT 3 shipped and turned a "three-to-four-years-out" concept into something we could build now.

Leadership wanted to unveil it at our sales conference. That gave us six weeks to go from idea to demo-ready prototype — so we scoped tightly to email replies, using the buyer's incoming message as grounding context for the AI draft.

Project highlights
Challenge

Bridging the gap

Reps needed AI help that sounded like their own voice and answered real prospect questions — not a generic chatbot reply.

Approach

Experience comes first

Scoped tightly, grounded generation in proprietary content, and shipped a working prototype in six weeks flat.

Outcome

Leading with differentiation

The first GenAI feature in Outreach's product — shipped on four surfaces, and the template for every AI feature since.

Designing under a deadline

My first instinct — a split button tucked in the corner of the compose window — matched patterns from Microsoft Copilot and GrammarlyGO. It didn't fit us. Users wanted to set tone, length, and prompt before generating, almost every time, and our pattern forced two levels of navigation to get there. Placement was inconsistent too: it didn't sit naturally alongside templates and snippets, and broke down across Gmail, Outlook, and our Chrome extension.

With the deadline closing in, we stepped back and wrote three principles instead: consistency (one toolbar, four surfaces), efficiency over distraction (faster than typing, or it's not worth shipping), and transparency & guidance (teach good prompting in the moment, and always ask users to review AI output before sending).

Five real challenges

Accuracy

One good draft wasn't realistic

Data Science told us a single accurate suggestion wasn't achievable yet. We shifted to three drafts — a "human in the loop" model I worried would slow people down. Research said the opposite: choosing between options felt like control, not friction.

Cost

Generation isn't free

Every input and output token has a real cost. We made generation opt-in per reply rather than automatic, so it triggered only when the AI actually earned its cost.

Competition

The floor moved overnight

Days after our conference debut, Microsoft announced Copilot and Google announced Bard. A first-mover moment suddenly had a lot of company.

Grounding

Public LLMs can't answer private questions

"Why is your product better than X?" isn't answered anywhere on the open internet. We built a content-recommendation layer that grounded drafts in each org's own templates and snippets — a moat competitors couldn't copy overnight.

Privacy

Trust had to be designed, not assumed

Legal required opt-in, not on-by-default. We built admin controls to toggle access per profile, plus clear in-product messaging that no personal or customer data is shared with ChatGPT.

"If you want to go fast, go alone. If you want to go far, go with your peers." That's exactly how this shipped — Product, Design, Engineering, Data Science, and Marketing, all in the same six weeks.

What I'd tell another designer starting this today

  • Shadow the real workflow before you design anything.
  • A tight deadline is a forcing function for principles, not just pixels.
  • "Human in the loop" is often a feature, not a compromise.
  • Your real moat is rarely the model — it's the proprietary context only you have.
  • Trust is a design problem. Opt-in doesn't have to mean low adoption.
